## This month in CSSWG

July was a design-debate month more than a resolution month: five telecons
(July 1, 8, 15, 22, 29) produced twenty-one resolutions plus two async —
including a brand-new `:nav-source` pseudo-class, `flip-self-*` keywords for
the `<select>` picker, and a compat-driven revert of the `flex` shorthand's
omitted basis to `0%` — but the arcs that matter to developers played out in
threads: the customizable `<select>` hover model got reopened, CSS mixins grew
a hygiene/privacy design, and `border-shape` hit the "is this the right
primitive?" wall. Scroll-driven animations, meanwhile, had a quietly
productive Interop-2026 cleanup, and the July 29 telecon closed the month
by pinning down long-open value and scroll-snap edge cases.

## Notable this month

- **Customizable `<select>`: the `:hover` top-layer rules are back on the table** ([#14143](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14143))
  - **Background:** an earlier resolution made `:hover`/`:active` chains break at top-layer boundaries, so a `<select>` stops looking hovered while its picker is open. [emilio](../people/emilio.md) reopened it: native selects don't behave that way, and the model is inconsistent with how `mouseenter`/`mouseleave` work.
  - **Why it matters:** this decides whether the button of a customizable `<select>` can visually react while the picker is open — a styling question every adopter of customizable select hits. WebKit [already ships the current behavior](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14143#issuecomment-4926522156); Gecko doesn't yet, so the window to change it is closing.
  - **Summary:** a genuine cross-vendor split, no resolution. jakearchibald showed that even a custom `<button>` [can't receive hover styles](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14143#issuecomment-4914908926) and [wished the select button were a sibling pseudo-element](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14143#issuecomment-4914421419); josepharhar [defended the current magic](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14143#issuecomment-4916743511) (authors shouldn't need `:not(:open)` everywhere); [mfreed7](../people/mfreed7.md) framed top-layer elements as ["pretend they're the last child of `<body>`"](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14143#issuecomment-4960920164), to which [emilio](../people/emilio.md) countered that the pretence is [rendering-only — events and selection don't play along](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14143#issuecomment-4994298091). The [July 9 telecon punted](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14143#issuecomment-4926816126) ([dbaron](../people/dbaron.md) wanted Mason Freed present). In parallel, jakearchibald proposed a [better default height for the picker](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14172), which josepharhar [supports](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14172#issuecomment-5132067918) — he had earlier called Chrome's current `min-block-size` a mistake — while [fantasai](../people/fantasai.md) [pinned down the core problem](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14172#issuecomment-5147819253): `max-block-size: stretch` resolves against the `position-area`-restricted containing block so the picker never flips when expected, and proposed `calc(100svb - 8px)` instead. The picker's positioning did land something concrete: July 1 [RESOLVED to add `flip-self-inline`/`flip-self-block` to `position-try` and use them for `::picker(select)`](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14062#issuecomment-4857805306), so the picker flips using its own writing mode.
  - **Related:** [Anchor Positioning](../features/anchor-positioning.md), [#11838](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11838) (`field-sizing: content` in the base-appearance stylesheet), [#13541](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13541) (June's position-fallback groundwork)
- **CSS mixins: `@locals` hygiene design, and whether `@mixin` survives as a name** ([#14004](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14004))
  - **Background:** custom functions and mixins are both in the `css-mixins-1` draft (the original umbrella proposal [was closed as accepted](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/9350) this month). [LeaVerou](../people/lea.md) proposed making today's pseudo-private `--_var` convention real: an `@locals` block whose variables are hygienically rewritten in place, private without a dummy mixin.
  - **Why it matters:** this is the "Sass features, natively" wave. Whether component libraries can actually hide internals — from the cascade, from devtools enumeration, from JS — decides how useful native mixins are compared to preprocessors.
  - **Summary:** the direction has broad support and the month hammered out its semantics: [tabatkins](../people/tabatkins.md) wants the local properties themselves hidden but is [fine exposing the rewritten (unguessable) names](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14004#issuecomment-5005879146); Lea flagged [devtools-style enumeration as a leak](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14004#issuecomment-5005846211) and wants [`@locals` at the sheet top level too](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14004#issuecomment-5047834293); [kbabbitt](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14004#issuecomment-5050748895) models locals as hoisted secret-name declarations; [keithamus](../people/keithamus.md) asked for [minifier-elision guarantees](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14004#issuecomment-5022611567). The naming stakes surfaced in [#13727](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13727): with these adopted, [tabatkins](../people/tabatkins.md) says [the separate `@mixin` rule could be dropped entirely](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13727#issuecomment-4986104794); Lea insists [`@macro` should go instead — `@mixin` is the term of art](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13727#issuecomment-5003846645). Ergonomics kept moving: parameter-less mixins now [serialize without parentheses](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13015#issuecomment-5095551489) (async RESOLVED, July 27), `!important` was [declared invalid in function/mixin parameters](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14212#issuecomment-5040244897), and comma-separated `@apply` lists were [rejected for conflicting with contents blocks](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14194#issuecomment-5006161341).
  - **Related:** [#12417](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/12417) (where `@mixin` may appear), [#14007](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14007)
- **`border-shape` growing pains: what strokes a custom border?** ([#13506](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13506))
  - **Background:** the single-shape `border-shape` takes its stroke width and color from one border side's properties. For shapes like [una](../people/una.md)'s scalloped cards — a 2px stroke on a ~20px-deep shape — that coupling forces padding hacks, so [noamr](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13506) proposed `border-shape-stroke-*` override properties.
  - **Why it matters:** `corner-shape`/`border-shape` is the new fancy-borders surface and has shipped in only one engine — the design is still movable, and how it lands decides how far CSS borders go toward SVG-style stroked drawing.
  - **Summary:** the [July 8 telecon discussed it without resolving](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13506#issuecomment-4917242913), and the thread became the month's biggest design fight. Lea argued the recurring footguns [call the whole primitive into question](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13506#issuecomment-4917291620) ("there is still time to completely redesign"); [tabatkins](../people/tabatkins.md) countered that anything short of [the full stroke property set (miterlimit and all) is incomplete](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13506#issuecomment-4917390965); noamr floated [`border-style: stroke` as the opt-in](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13506#issuecomment-4933932345) and [tabatkins](../people/tabatkins.md) [sketched how it would compose](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13506#issuecomment-4939883382). Scope got trimmed around the edges: Lea proposed [deferring the two-shape variant to L5](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14150#issuecomment-4928994730) (noamr and [tabatkins pushed back](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14150#issuecomment-4929394205)), the `corner` shorthand's slash-parsing worries [mostly dissolved once kbabbitt dug up the earlier resolution](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13889#issuecomment-4975179105), and the interaction with classic border properties got its own issue ([#14149](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14149)). Month-end, [tabatkins](../people/tabatkins.md) [queued the full stroke-property set for `border-shape-*` for discussion](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13506#issuecomment-5124200452) — the "all or nothing" position, now on the agenda.
  - **Related:** [#14147](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14147) — an overflow-safe `shape-inside` hitting the same "shapes meet the box model" wall: [cyclic sizing](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14147#issuecomment-4938091362) vs [tabatkins' stretchy-shapes idea](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14147#issuecomment-4930344454)
- **Scroll-driven animations: an Interop-2026 alignment month** ([#11469](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11469))
  - **Background:** scroll-animations-1 and web-animations-2 edge cases are being flushed out by Interop 2026 test work, with WebKit and Gecko implementations catching up to Chromium's shipped behavior — see [Scroll-driven Animations](../features/scroll-driven-animations.md).
  - **Why it matters:** each of these changes observable Web Animations API behavior for anyone scripting scroll/view timelines — and they mark the spec converging on what can interoperably ship.
  - **Summary:** six resolutions landed. Setting `rangeStart`/`rangeEnd` to values that need an element to resolve is now [a parse error](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13853#issuecomment-4857950246) (July 1); `play()` on a scroll-driven animation [re-aligns a sticky start time to the scroll range](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11469#issuecomment-4916924373) (July 8); `ViewTimelineOptions.inset` defaults were settled as ["spec is right, fix the tests"](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11644#issuecomment-4982811693), as was [coordinated-value-list shorthand serialization](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13500#issuecomment-4982841033) (July 15); and auto-aligning a start time now [applies a pending playback rate](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14173#issuecomment-5026368479) (async, July 20). And the `ViewTimeline` constructor [now takes a non-optional dictionary with a required `element`](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13959#issuecomment-5120698511) (July 29) — the minutes record weinig framing it as an IDL contradiction (a non-nullable `subject` attribute you could nonetheless construct as null) and ydaniv noting a subject-less `ViewTimeline` is practically useless, so the constructor got stricter instead. Still moving: [BorisChiou flagged](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11477#issuecomment-5007620612) the `CSSKeywordish` typing of `inset` as interop-blocking, named-timeline re-lookup timing is queued ([#13944](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13944)), and a fresh proposal asks [how `zoom` interacts with `animation-range`](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14188).
  - **Related:** [Scroll-driven Animations](../features/scroll-driven-animations.md), [#14205](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14205) (`rangeStart`/`rangeEnd` IDL fix)
- **Masonry (`grid-lanes`): filling in the corners, and the reading-order question returns** ([#13931](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13931))
  - **Background:** after the 2025-11 `display: grid-lanes` decision, spec work is now detail-filling — see [CSS Masonry](../features/masonry.md).
  - **Why it matters:** the details being decided now (gaps, gap decorations, out-of-flow behavior, reading order) are what will make masonry usable — or an accessibility hazard — when it ships.
  - **Summary:** the July 22 telecon [resolved that static positions of out-of-flow items reverse under `fill-reverse`](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13931#issuecomment-5048621763), following flexbox precedent. css-gaps opened a five-issue family defining how gaps and gap decorations work in grid-lanes containers ([#14153](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14153), [#14154](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14154), [#14155](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14155), [#14156](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14156), [#14169](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14169)). And the long-running [accessibility thread on reading order](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/5675) woke up: [tabatkins](../people/tabatkins.md) proposed a "reading region" algorithm [approximating the reading flows implied by layout](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/5675#issuecomment-4962909253), [rachelandrew](../people/rachelandrew.md) found the rules sensible, and accessibility advocate nattarnoff [returned to review the proposals](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/5675#issuecomment-5035023037).
  - **Related:** [CSS Masonry](../features/masonry.md), [#10884](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/10884) (`item-slack` naming), [#13512](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13512) (`grid-auto-flow: normal` compat risk measured at ~3% of pageloads)
- **`:nav-source`: style the element that started a navigation** ([#11801](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11801))
  - **Background:** [bramus](../people/bramus.md) proposed a pseudo-class for the element that initiated the outgoing navigation, mirroring the Navigation API's `sourceElement`; css-navigation-1 drafted it as a links-only `:trigger-link`.
  - **Why it matters:** unlike `:active`, this state *stays on* until the navigation completes — exactly what you need to keep the clicked card or link highlighted while a cross-document view transition is under way.
  - **Summary:** the July 1 telecon [resolved twice](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/11801#issuecomment-4857772799): the pseudo-class applies not just to links (forms and submit buttons also trigger navigations), and it is named `:nav-source`.
  - **Related:** css-navigation-1 — a young module now carrying its first author-facing selector
- **`::first-line` / `::first-letter` won't apply inside text inputs** ([#13926](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13926))
  - **Background:** Blink half-accidentally supported `::first-line` on `input`/`textarea`; the internal layout of form controls is unspecced and differs across engines, so behavior there was never interoperable.
  - **Why it matters:** an author-visible selector rule is now explicit: these pseudo-elements do nothing on text inputs, the same way `::before`/`::after` don't apply — one less cross-browser surprise.
  - **Summary:** [RESOLVED July 15](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13926#issuecomment-4983249817): they don't apply "for now", to be revisited once form-control internals have defined layout. In the call, lea pressed the author-expectation side while [emilio](../people/emilio.md) argued there's simply no interoperable layout to hook them onto.
  - **Related:** [#14190](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14190) — the same "spec the form-control layout" push in reverse: making `align-content` center a block-container `<button>`'s content, with a [Gecko prototype already up](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14190#issuecomment-5004765963)
- **`flex: 1` means `flex-basis: 0%` again — the spec bows to compat** ([#5742](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/5742))
  - **Background:** the [`flex` shorthand](../features/flex-shorthand.md)'s omitted basis was originally `0%`, then respecified as `0` in 2015 — but implementations never followed, and the open question had been flagged on the feature's page since it was written up.
  - **Why it matters:** `flex: 1` is on practically every flexbox on the web, and `0%` and `0` behave differently in indefinite-main-size containers. The spec now matches what every engine actually does, so the longstanding "spec says `0`, browsers do `0%`" trap disappears.
  - **Summary:** [RESOLVED July 29](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/5742#issuecomment-5120741577): switch the omitted `flex-basis` back to `0%` for compat, with a spec note about it. The minutes record [tabatkins](../people/tabatkins.md) summarizing the decade-long arc: `0%` was chosen "specifically to trigger indeterminate behavior", the later `0px` change was never adopted by implementations, and "at this point, we're fairly certain it can't be changed" — sites depend on `0%`.
  - **Related:** [the `flex` shorthand](../features/flex-shorthand.md) — July's `/query` page traced this exact history
- **`calc-mix()` learns to simplify like `calc()`** ([#13839](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13839))
  - **Background:** weinig hit missing simplification rules while implementing css-values-5's `calc-mix()` and proposed a set in the issue.
  - **Why it matters:** simplification is observable — it is what serialization and `getComputedStyle()` return — so this defines the first interop surface of the new mix notation before two implementations diverge.
  - **Summary:** [RESOLVED July 29](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13839#issuecomment-5120811698): `calc-mix()` simplifies exactly as `calc()` would, except the mixed-type case resolves to `calc(0px + 0%)`. The minutes record [tabatkins](../people/tabatkins.md) framing `calc-mix()` as a convenience for linear interpolation that should drop `0%` terms the way `calc()` drops products — but never simplify away to nothing.
  - **Related:** css-values-5 — the module also carrying `random()` and `sibling-index()`
- **`getComputedStyle()` on view-transition pseudos that don't exist** ([#13904](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13904))
  - **Background:** engines disagreed on what `getComputedStyle()` should return for `::view-transition-*` pseudo-elements that don't currently exist — a WPT test invented an inheritance tree, and Chrome crashed on some queries.
  - **Why it matters:** these pseudos are how view-transition authors debug; what a non-existent one reports is now defined instead of engine-dependent.
  - **Summary:** [RESOLVED July 1](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13904#issuecomment-4858013386): non-existing pseudo-elements inherit from their ultimate originating element when queried. Follow-ups settled an author concern — martrapp [relied on non-existent pseudos reporting a non-`px` width](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13904#issuecomment-4912524842), and [emilio](../people/emilio.md) confirmed [that doesn't change](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13904#issuecomment-4913895964): `width` isn't inherited, and a boxless pseudo still reports `auto`.
  - **Related:** [#14179](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14179) (scoped view transitions: [async resolution proposed July 27](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14179#issuecomment-5095755511) — `startViewTransition` throws for elements that can't have layout containment), [#13428](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13428) (scoped-VT capture vs the a11y tree)
- **Scroll snap pins down its edge cases: single-axis scrollers, joined axes, fragmentation** ([#14018](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14018))
  - **Background:** the group previously resolved to allow true single-axis scrollers (one axis `scroll`, the other `clip` no longer coerced); specs that assumed "a scroller scrolls both axes" are being updated.
  - **Why it matters:** snap behavior in nested or one-axis scroller setups becomes predictable: a snap position now attaches, per axis, to the nearest ancestor that actually scrolls that axis.
  - **Summary:** [RESOLVED July 22](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14018#issuecomment-5048738521): "Snap positions affect the nearest ancestor scroll container in each axis they align to." The same telecon also resolved that coincident snap candidates [tie-break in flat-tree order](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13832#issuecomment-5048660954), so shadow-DOM slotting no longer changes which element you snap to. The July 29 telecon kept going: a keyword (name TBD) will opt into [joined-axis snapping](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/9519#issuecomment-5121002451) — snap to an element in both axes or not at all, where today the axes always snap independently (the minutes record [flackr](../people/flackr.md) arguing that for a two-axis snap target "the intent is usually to snap to the element entirely, or not at all") — and [fragmented snap targets snap their bounding box](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13490#issuecomment-5120957622), with alignment adjusted (details TBD) to keep the element in view when it is bigger than the viewport, resolving what multicol WPT failures exposed.
  - **Related:** [#13816](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13816#issuecomment-4917114479) (July 8: scroll direction of inputs follows the element's `direction`, closed no change)
