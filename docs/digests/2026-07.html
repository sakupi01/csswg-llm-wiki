This month in CSSWG

July was a design-debate month more than a resolution month: three telecons (July 8, 15, 22) produced ten resolutions plus one async, but the arcs that matter to developers played out in threads — the customizable <select> hover model got reopened, CSS mixins grew a hygiene/privacy design, and border-shape hit the "is this the right primitive?" wall. Scroll-driven animations, meanwhile, had a quietly productive Interop-2026 cleanup. (Mirror data synced through 2026-07-23; anything later lands in the next weekly digest.)

Notable this month

  • Customizable <select>: the :hover top-layer rules are back on the table (#14143)
    • Background: an earlier resolution made :hover/:active chains break at top-layer boundaries, so a <select> stops looking hovered while its picker is open. emilio reopened it: native selects don't behave that way, and the model is inconsistent with how mouseenter/mouseleave work.
    • Why it matters: this decides whether the button of a customizable <select> can visually react while the picker is open — a styling question every adopter of customizable select hits. WebKit already ships the current behavior; Gecko doesn't yet, so the window to change it is closing.
    • Summary: a genuine cross-vendor split, no resolution. jakearchibald showed that even a custom <button> can't receive hover styles and wished the select button were a sibling pseudo-element; josepharhar defended the current magic (authors shouldn't need :not(:open) everywhere); mfreed7 framed top-layer elements as "pretend they're the last child of <body>", to which emilio countered that the pretence is rendering-only — events and selection don't play along. The July 9 telecon punted (dbaron wanted Mason Freed present). In parallel, jakearchibald proposed a better default height for the picker, and josepharhar called Chrome's current min-block-size a mistake.
    • Related: #11838 (field-sizing: content in the base-appearance stylesheet), #13541 (June's position-fallback groundwork)
  • CSS mixins: @locals hygiene design, and whether @mixin survives as a name (#14004)
    • Background: custom functions and mixins are both in the css-mixins-1 draft (the original umbrella proposal was closed as accepted this month). LeaVerou proposed making today's pseudo-private --_var convention real: an @locals block whose variables are hygienically rewritten in place, private without a dummy mixin.
    • Why it matters: this is the "Sass features, natively" wave. Whether component libraries can actually hide internals — from the cascade, from devtools enumeration, from JS — decides how useful native mixins are compared to preprocessors.
    • Summary: the direction has broad support and the month hammered out its semantics: tabatkins wants the local properties themselves hidden but is fine exposing the rewritten (unguessable) names; Lea flagged devtools-style enumeration as a leak and wants @locals at the sheet top level too; kbabbitt models locals as hoisted secret-name declarations; keithamus asked for minifier-elision guarantees. The naming stakes surfaced in #13727: with these adopted, tabatkins says the separate @mixin rule could be dropped entirely; Lea insists @macro should go instead — @mixin is the term of art. Ergonomics kept moving: parameter-less mixins are heading to an async resolution to serialize without parentheses, !important was declared invalid in function/mixin parameters, and comma-separated @apply lists were rejected for conflicting with contents blocks.
    • Related: #12417 (where @mixin may appear), #14007
  • border-shape growing pains: what strokes a custom border? (#13506)
    • Background: the single-shape border-shape takes its stroke width and color from one border side's properties. For shapes like una's scalloped cards — a 2px stroke on a ~20px-deep shape — that coupling forces padding hacks, so noamr proposed border-shape-stroke-* override properties.
    • Why it matters: corner-shape/border-shape is the new fancy-borders surface and has shipped in only one engine — the design is still movable, and how it lands decides how far CSS borders go toward SVG-style stroked drawing.
    • Summary: the July 8 telecon discussed it without resolving, and the thread became the month's biggest design fight. Lea argued the recurring footguns call the whole primitive into question ("there is still time to completely redesign"); tabatkins countered that anything short of the full stroke property set (miterlimit and all) is incomplete; noamr floated border-style: stroke as the opt-in and tabatkins sketched how it would compose. Scope got trimmed around the edges: Lea proposed deferring the two-shape variant to L5 (noamr and tabatkins pushed back), the corner shorthand's slash-parsing worries mostly dissolved once kbabbitt dug up the earlier resolution, and the interaction with classic border properties got its own issue (#14149).
    • Related: #14147 — an overflow-safe shape-inside hitting the same "shapes meet the box model" wall: cyclic sizing vs tabatkins' stretchy-shapes idea
  • Scroll-driven animations: an Interop-2026 alignment month (#11469)
    • Background: scroll-animations-1 and web-animations-2 edge cases are being flushed out by Interop 2026 test work, with WebKit and Gecko implementations catching up to Chromium's shipped behavior — see Scroll-driven Animations.
    • Why it matters: each of these changes observable Web Animations API behavior for anyone scripting scroll/view timelines — and they mark the spec converging on what can interoperably ship.
    • Summary: four resolutions landed. play() on a scroll-driven animation re-aligns a sticky start time to the scroll range (July 8); ViewTimelineOptions.inset defaults were settled as "spec is right, fix the tests", as was coordinated-value-list shorthand serialization (July 15); and auto-aligning a start time now applies a pending playback rate (async, July 20). Still moving: BorisChiou flagged the CSSKeywordish typing of inset as interop-blocking, named-timeline re-lookup timing is queued (#13944), and two fresh proposals target sticky positioning: a stuck range name and how zoom interacts with animation-range.
    • Related: Scroll-driven Animations, #14205 (rangeStart/rangeEnd IDL fix)
  • Masonry (grid-lanes): filling in the corners, and the reading-order question returns (#13931)
    • Background: after the 2025-11 display: grid-lanes decision, spec work is now detail-filling — see CSS Masonry.
    • Why it matters: the details being decided now (gaps, gap decorations, out-of-flow behavior, reading order) are what will make masonry usable — or an accessibility hazard — when it ships.
    • Summary: the July 22 telecon resolved that static positions of out-of-flow items reverse under fill-reverse, following flexbox precedent. css-gaps opened a five-issue family defining how gaps and gap decorations work in grid-lanes containers (#14153, #14154, #14155, #14156, #14169). And the long-running accessibility thread on reading order woke up: tabatkins proposed a "reading region" algorithm approximating human reading flow, rachelandrew found the rules sensible, and accessibility advocate nattarnoff returned to review the proposals.
    • Related: CSS Masonry, #10884 (item-slack naming), #13512 (grid-auto-flow: normal compat risk measured at ~3% of pageloads)
  • ::first-line / ::first-letter won't apply inside text inputs (#13926)
    • Background: Blink half-accidentally supported ::first-line on input/textarea; the internal layout of form controls is unspecced and differs across engines, so behavior there was never interoperable.
    • Why it matters: an author-visible selector rule is now explicit: these pseudo-elements do nothing on text inputs, the same way ::before/::after don't apply — one less cross-browser surprise.
    • Summary: RESOLVED July 15: they don't apply "for now", to be revisited once form-control internals have defined layout. In the call, lea pressed the author-expectation side while emilio argued there's simply no interoperable layout to hook them onto.
    • Related: #14190 — the same "spec the form-control layout" push in reverse: making align-content center a block-container <button>'s content, with a Gecko prototype already up
  • Scroll snap learns about single-axis scrollers (#14018)
    • Background: the group previously resolved to allow true single-axis scrollers (one axis scroll, the other clip no longer coerced); specs that assumed "a scroller scrolls both axes" are being updated.
    • Why it matters: snap behavior in nested or one-axis scroller setups becomes predictable: a snap position now attaches, per axis, to the nearest ancestor that actually scrolls that axis.
    • Summary: RESOLVED July 22: "Snap positions affect the nearest ancestor scroll container in each axis they align to." The same telecon also resolved that coincident snap candidates tie-break in flat-tree order, so shadow-DOM slotting no longer changes which element you snap to.
    • Related: #13816 (July 8: scroll direction of inputs follows the element's direction, closed no change)

Still contested

  • #13980 how does ::interest-button turn on? — still open after June's reversal; LeaVerou returned with new arguments for showing the affordance where expressing interest is cumbersome; the July 9 telecon didn't resolve.
  • #10001 wildcard selectors for utility classes — LeaVerou pushes .foo-*; Loirooriol and Crissov raise grammar and attribute-selector conflicts; the adjacent @selector proposal (#14180) is drawing skepticism.
  • #14112 anchor positioning: what happens on scroll? — anchored elements left behind by scrolling; part of the Anchor Positioning story.
  • #3658 system-ui is undesired for some users — loud thread, demoted here: largely a Windows CJK platform-font problem (jfkthame: "simply a platform font bug").
  • css-color conversion-precision cluster — #14133 zero chroma for powerless hue, #14134 carrying-forward step order, #14070 omissible alpha in relative-color serialization, #14207 DeltaE OK2 — spec-internal precision work with little author-visible impact so far.

  • Selectors 4 CR push (#12799): horizontal review is complete and an updated WD is being prepared; fantasai reviewed the changes at the July 22 telecon and noted css-syntax-3 should republish first so the an+b grammar links resolve.
