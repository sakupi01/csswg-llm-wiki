## This month in CSSWG

June's throughline was **implementations catching up to specs, and specs
reacting**. CSS Color moved toward Candidate Recommendation and immediately drew
a wave of byte-exact serialization questions; scroll-driven animations got a
batch of timing fixes; and the month's sharpest moment was the Working Group
*reversing its own resolution* on how `::interest-button` should turn on. 28
resolutions landed across ~92 active agenda items.

## Themes

### `::interest-button` reverses course on turning itself on

The month's most instructive arc. `::interest-button` is the pseudo-element that
surfaces an affordance for interest invokers (`interestfor`). Early in June the
WG resolved to gate it behind an **inline media condition** —
[use `display: if(media())` to enable it on the right systems](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13980#issuecomment-4682270794).
lwarlow argued UAs should keep latitude ("the spec should give UAs the freedom to
enable or disable it") and that fears of layout breakage were unrealistic. Weeks
later the group **[voided that resolution](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13980#issuecomment-4801238759)**:
[LeaVerou](../people/lea.md), after developer conversations at CSS Day, reported authors would simply
turn it off — anything that affects layout by default breaks pages, and an
opt-out adds more UX noise (stray tab stops, tooltips intermixed with real
targets) than it solves. Net: whether and how `::interest-button` is on by
default is open again ([#13980](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13980), 37 comments this month).
The tension — UA latitude vs. "layout-affecting defaults will be disabled" — is
the one to watch as interest invokers ship.

### Scroll-driven animations get a semantics pass

A cluster of precision fixes for scroll/view timelines, individually small but
together removing a batch of "why did my scroll animation jump?" foot-guns:
[timeline-source changes now apply asynchronously](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13480#issuecomment-4614087368);
[auto-rewind has no effect when the timeline is unresolved](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13817#issuecomment-4614196831),
paired with a new [`play-always` trigger that always rewinds](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13643#issuecomment-4613983278);
[`startOffset`/`endOffset` became nullable](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13844#issuecomment-4614158334);
and [scroll-timeline names are now loosely matched](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13974#issuecomment-4672119742).

### CSS Color 5: shipping forces the exact answers (still contested)

Color 4 advanced to CRD and Color 5 was republished as a WD mid-month, and the
practical consequence was a burst of "what *exactly* serializes?" threads — most
still open. How `color-mix()` parses and whether the sum-to-zero tests move
rather than delete ([#13996](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13996)); the resolved value and
serialization of `alpha()` in relative color ([#13994](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13994)); and
whether alpha is omissible in relative-color serialization — sent to async with
[svgeesus noting "we don't have consensus"](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14070) ([#14070](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14070)).
Example 32 was flagged as outdated against the newer `none`/powerless-component
model ([#14100](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14100), 19 comments). In parallel, Color 4's static
gamut-mapping algorithm is now being implemented (Firefox) and benchmarked
([#10579](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/10579)). The pattern: with engines shipping, the group
is pinning down behavior that used to be "close enough."

### New surfaces reach milestones

[Link Params was published as a First Public Working Draft](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14028#issuecomment-4672083352)
([#14028](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14028)), with its `param()` function
[required to carry at least two values](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13767#issuecomment-4732890422).
Randomness matured in Values 5 — resolutions on
[how `random()` evaluates inside arbitrary substitution](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13662#issuecomment-4672428004)
and on [`sibling-index()`/`sibling-count()` failure behavior](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13736#issuecomment-4791604896),
while [`random-item()` weighting](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/14079) wrestled with a
syntax overlap (`block 20%` is ambiguous) and leaned toward an `if()`-like
pattern. And forms/anchor-positioning groundwork landed: the `<select>`
[position-try fallbacks were redefined](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13541#issuecomment-4732677022)
and [`@position-try` gained globally-scoped names like `@keyframes`](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/13567#issuecomment-4791554191)
— the runway for July's `flip-self-*` keywords.
